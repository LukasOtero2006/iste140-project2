Wellbeing Tools Compared

Seven tools, one table

There are two kinds of help for screen time: the tools already built into your phone, and apps you install yourself. The table below compares two built-in tools and five popular apps on platform, cost, features, how they try to change your behavior, and what data they collect. All information was checked on 20 November 2026; prices are in US dollars and can differ by country and store, so check the current price before you buy.

My ratings are personal judgments for a student on a budget, based on cost, how well each tool fits studying, and how much data it appears to collect.

My recommendation for a first-year student during exams

For a first-year student in the exam period, I would combine three layers that cost almost nothing. The first layer is the free built-in tool, Screen Time on iPhone or Digital Wellbeing on Android. I would use its Downtime or Bedtime mode so the phone quiets down at night, because phone use at bedtime is linked to getting too little sleep (Carter et al., 2016), and I would add an app limit for the one or two apps that waste the most time (Apple Inc., n.d.-a; Google, n.d.-a). The second layer is ScreenZen or one sec on the single app where you scroll without thinking. Both are free for at least one app, and a short pause targets the automatic habit instead of blocking everything (WhistleOut, n.d.-a; WhistleOut, n.d.-b). That pause may lose some power after a few weeks (WhistleOut, n.d.-a), but an exam period is short enough for it to help. The third layer is Forest for study sessions, because the growing tree gives a small reason to leave the phone alone, and its Allow List can block other apps on iPhone (SEEKRTECH, n.d.-a). I would skip Opal Pro at $99.99 per year (Autonomous, n.d.) and Freedom Premium at $39.99 per year (Freedom, n.d.) for now, since a student budget is tight and the free tools cover most of the need. The exception is Freedom if the laptop is your biggest distraction, because it is the only tool here that blocks across phone and computer together (Asian Efficiency, 2026). Keep in mind that any app on the phone can be switched off by someone determined enough (Autonomous, n.d.), so also leave the phone in another room during study blocks. Finally, test the combination in the 7-day experiment and keep only the tools that make a real difference.
